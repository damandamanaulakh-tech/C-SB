# 10 FUNCTIONAL SEGMENTS (full wording)

SEG-01  Biological Regulation and Internal State  
Human cognition is continually influenced by arousal, sleep, energy balance, stress regulation and internal bodily signals. Interoception includes sensing, interpreting and integrating signals from inside the body; research links it with affect, selfhood, regulation and decision-making.

SEG-02  Perception and Body Representation  
Perception is not passive recording. Sensory input is selected, combined with prior expectations and interpreted in relation to goals, body state and context.

SEG-03  Sensorimotor Action and Physical Execution  
Human action involves preparation, selection, prediction, bodily control, feedback and correction. Sensorimotor research treats behaviour as more than muscle movement; it includes preparation, learning, agency, control and integration with cognition.

SEG-04  Attention and Executive Control  
Executive functions are commonly decomposed into working memory, inhibitory control and cognitive flexibility, with higher-order planning and reasoning built from their interaction. Stress, sleep and emotional conditions can alter these functions.

SEG-05  Learning, Memory and Knowledge  
Human memory is not one storage box. Research distinguishes working memory and multiple long-term systems, including episodic, semantic and procedural memory; expanded taxonomies also distinguish numerous explicit and implicit subtypes.

SEG-06  Reasoning, Planning, Decision and Creativity  
Reasoning and decision-making depend on attention, working memory, knowledge, emotional state, bodily signals and executive control. Emotional and cognitive routes interact particularly under risk and ambiguity rather than operating as completely independent systems.

SEG-07  Language and Communication  
Human language includes sound recognition, meaning, grammar, comprehension, production, prediction, pragmatics and social adjustment. Comprehension and production interact rather than functioning as completely separate pipelines.

SEG-08  Emotion, Motivation, Intent and Motive  
NIMH separates positive-valence and negative-valence systems because reward, threat, loss, frustration and approach/avoidance involve distinguishable processes. Emotion includes experiential, physiological, cognitive and behavioural components.

SEG-09  Consciousness, Self and Social Intelligence  
Research treats metacognition, consciousness, empathy, theory of mind and social cognition as related but distinguishable processes. Empathy itself is not unitary, and theory of mind involves modelling others’ beliefs and intentions. Theories of consciousness remain actively debated; no single complete theory has been scientifically closed.

SEG-10  Development, Metacognition and Adaptation  
Metacognition includes monitoring and controlling one’s own perception, memory, decisions and reasoning. Human development also depends on biological maturation, experience, teaching, imitation, cultural transmission and environmental conditions.
